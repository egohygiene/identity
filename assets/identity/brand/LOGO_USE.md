## Permitted identification and integration

You may copy, display, distribute and embed the covered artwork to identify,
discuss or integrate the actual Ego Hygiene Identity tool. Commercial and
noncommercial contexts are permitted. Examples include documentation, articles,
package listings, application launchers and integration interfaces.

You may resize, rasterize, convert to glyph outlines, use a monochrome rendition
or make comparable presentation adaptations for those identifying uses. You may
bundle and redistribute a covered glyph in an icon font, including a Nerd
Fonts-style integration, with the policy and credit supplied in the package.
These permissions do not imply that a particular font project has accepted the
glyph or that its own distribution terms are automatically compatible.

## Distinct derivatives for other projects

You may adapt covered artwork for a different project, including a commercial
project, when the result uses a different name and a genuinely distinct visual
identity that does not reasonably suggest official Identity branding or
affiliation with Ego Hygiene.

Resizing, changing format or recoloring the existing mark alone does not turn
it into an unrelated product's own brand. Do not use the unchanged logo, a
confusingly similar rendition, or the Identity/Ego Hygiene name as the source
identity of another tool. Do not claim official status, maintenance, sponsorship
or endorsement that has not been granted. Truthful references to the actual
Identity tool remain permitted as described above.

## Credit and redistribution

Retain this policy with redistributed logo or glyph files and carry this credit:

For bundled icons, fonts or integrations, a package notice, README, metadata or
about page may carry the credit; it need not appear beside every rendered icon.
An adaptation must not be presented as an unchanged official master. Credit
does not imply endorsement.

these conditions or relabel the covered originals under another license. Check
the receiving package/font terms before combining them; incompatible terms
need a separately reviewed permission rather than an assumed exception.
